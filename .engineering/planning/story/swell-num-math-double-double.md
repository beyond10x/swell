## Context

Slice 4 of 19, `numbers-and-dates` (`epic:slice-04-numbers-and-dates`; `docs/design/engine.md`, section 12, item 4; decision 6, the bullets "`Math`", "The functions of `Math` that IEEE 754 does not fix" and "Which `f64` methods an engine crate may call"). This is the first of three stories that write the `Math` functions the standard library answers through the platform `libm`, in `swell-num`, in plain `f64` arithmetic, so that a script prints the same bytes on every platform (HOST-135). It is the foundation: the double-double arithmetic every kernel of the other two is written in, `hypot` (the one function that needs nothing else), and the two gates that keep the rest honest (`libm-check` and `math-data-check`). The design sentence names one story for all of it (`swell-num-math`); that is more than one session (about 3,500 lines of kernels, 9,000 lines of reference data and a procedure run once), so it is three stories: this one, `story:swell-num-math-exp-and-log` and `story:swell-num-math-trigonometry`, which depend on this one and not on each other. It lifts no test262 case; `story:math-functions` builds the `Math` builtins on the three and lifts the cases.

On the base (`c6d44d1`; the unit reads every place named in this story again on its base) `crates/num` has `date.rs`, `literal.rs`, `siphash.rs` and `to_string.rs` and no `f64` function but those of `Math`'s exact set (which are in `crates/swell/src/builtins/math.rs`); no `libm-check` and no `data/math/`. `data/unicode/17.0.0` with `SOURCE` and the task `unicode-data-check` (`taskfiles/unicode-data.yml`) are the model for the data gate. The gate `forbid-unsafe-check` (in `Taskfile.yml`) is the model for `libm-check`.

Design it follows, by name: `docs/design/engine.md` decision 6, "The functions of `Math` that IEEE 754 does not fix", the paragraph "Double-double" (`two_sum` of Knuth in 6 additions, `two_product` of Dekker with Veltkamp's split by 2^27 + 1 and `mul_add` not used, a power-of-two scaling at magnitude 2^996 or more, add, multiply, divide and square root of pairs, the root by one Newton step; each operation's relative error at most 2^-100; no x86 without SSE2: a `compile_error!`; rustc does not contract `a * b + c`), item 8 (`hypot`), the paragraph "The accuracy the engine promises" (group A, `hypot`: less than 1 ulp, `hypot(3, 4)` is 5) and "Which `f64` methods an engine crate may call" with its check; decision 2 (`swell-num` depends on nothing); decision 13.

**Not run, to be measured by the unit.** The design text on `Math` was written and not run: its numbers are claims. This unit measures, with the test's own exact arithmetic, the relative error of each double-double operation over 2,000 fixed pseudo-random inputs per operation (the generator is the multiplicative one the design uses for reference data: Knuth's 64-bit LCG, multiplier 6364136223846793005, increment 1442695040888963407, seed 1) and over the boundary inputs (subnormals, 2^996, 2^1023, the largest Number, signs, zeros); it states the largest measured error beside the design's 2^-100. The ceiling is 2^-100: a measured error above it returns the story to the coordinator, which does not pass its acceptance and which reports the difference before any kernel is written on it (the other two stories take the error from this measure); the tests assert the measured bound and also that it is at most 2^-100, a constant of the test, so a measure above the ceiling fails them.

**Prerequisite of the design author, not in the `Writes` of this story: the reference data of `Math`.** The design names one file `data/math/reference.txt` and one test `tests/math.rs`, and the work is cut in three stories. The sentence below is written into `docs/design/engine.md` by the design change that is merged into `wave/s10-1` before the unit starts, and the three stories follow it as written; the start gate of the acceptance is its first line. The sentence:

> **Reference data of Math** (decision 6, in the paragraph "How a test holds the bound with no second implementation", in place of a single file and its one test). Each of the three stories that write `Math` functions in `swell-num` commits its own reference file, in the line format and by the procedure of "The tool that makes the lines": `data/math/reference-hypot.txt` (`story:swell-num-math-double-double`: the lines of `hypot`, the only function it writes), `data/math/reference-exp-log.txt` (`story:swell-num-math-exp-and-log`) and `data/math/reference-trig.txt` (`story:swell-num-math-trigonometry`). Each file has one line `<sha256>  <file>` in `data/math/SOURCE`, which `math-data-check` reads, and is read by the test file of its story (`crates/num/tests/math_dd.rs`, `math_exp_log.rs`, `math_trig.rs`, modules of the crate's one test binary).

- `crates/num/src/math.rs` (new; `pub mod math;` in `crates/num/src/lib.rs`): the module root with `pub mod dd;` and `pub fn hypot`, and at its top `#[cfg(all(target_arch = "x86", not(target_feature = "sse2")))] compile_error!("swell-num needs SSE2 or a target without extended-precision f64 arithmetic");`. `crates/num/src/math/dd.rs` (new): `Dd { hi: f64, lo: f64 }`, `two_sum(a, b) -> Dd` (Knuth, 6 additions), `fast_two_sum` (for |a| >= |b|), `two_product(a, b) -> Dd` (Dekker: Veltkamp's split of each factor by `134217729.0` (2^27 + 1), the four partial products; for a factor of magnitude 2^996 or more both factors are scaled by 2^-30 and the product by 2^60 afterwards, so the split cannot overflow), `add`, `sub`, `mul`, `div` (the quotient of the high parts, one correction by the residual in double-double), `sqrt` (one Newton step on `f64::sqrt` of the high part), `neg`, `scale2(k: i32)` (multiplication by 2^k in at most two factors, each a Number, so that no intermediate overflows or flushes) and `to_f64` (`hi + lo`, the one rounding). Each operation is a short fixed sequence of `+ - * /` with no loop and no branch on a value but the scaling guard.
- `math::hypot(values: &[f64]) -> f64` over arguments that the caller has already coerced, in the order of the draft (21.3.2.18, `#sec-math.hypot`): an infinite argument gives +∞ (a NaN argument too), else a NaN gives NaN, else all zero gives +0; for the rest the arguments are scaled by 2^-e with e the largest binary exponent (a power of two: exact), squared by `two_product`, summed in double-double (Neumaier's compensated sum), the pair's square root taken and scaled back with `scale2`. A slice of one value is the absolute value; the empty slice gives +0. The loop is over the slice and is the caller's to charge (`swell-num` takes no budget).

## Context

Slice 4 of 19, `numbers-and-dates` (`epic:slice-04-numbers-and-dates`; `docs/design/engine.md` decision 6, the bullet "`Date.parse` and the String argument of `new Date`"). This story is the text of a date as pure functions of `swell-num`: reading the three forms of `Date.parse` and writing the strings of `toString`, `toDateString`, `toTimeString`, `toUTCString` and `toISOString`, tested without an engine, so that `story:date-text-formats` is wiring (the builtins, the charges, the hook for a date-time without an offset). It lifts no test262 case: its result is read by the story after it. It depends on `story:swell-num-calendar-and-local-time` (`DateFields`, `date_fields`, `make_day`, `make_time`, `make_date`, `parse_time_zone_offset_string`) and on nothing else; it writes only `crates/num`, so it shares no file with an open story of slice 10.

On the base (`c6d44d1`; the unit reads every place named in this story again on its base) `crates/num/src/date.rs` has no text of a date; `crates/swell/src/builtins/date.rs` does not exist (the clock story writes it with the two seams `parse_date_string`, which answers NaN, and `date_to_string`, which answers "Invalid Date"; the text story replaces both by calls of this story's functions).

Design it follows, by name: `docs/design/engine.md` decision 6, "`Date.parse` and the String argument of `new Date`" (the three forms, each the whole String: told apart by the first code unit (a digit, `+` or `-` the first form, a letter the second or third, by the fourth code unit: a space or a comma); every rule of range and of NaN is written there, and the work is one pass with no backtracking); ECMA-262 21.4.1.32 (`#sec-date-time-string-format`), `#sec-expanded-years`, `#sec-todatestring`, `#sec-date.prototype.toisostring`, `#sec-date.prototype.toutcstring`, the tables of day and month names (`#sec-todatestring-day-names`, `#sec-todatestring-month-names`); decision 13 (no panic).

## What later stories reuse

`story:date-text-formats` calls `parse_date_text`, `date_string`, `time_string`, `time_zone_string`, `utc_string` and `iso_string`; `DateText` is the whole interface. The functions take and give no engine type: text goes in as code units (`&[U]`, `U: Copy + Into<u32>`, as `literal.rs` does) and out as `String` (ASCII only).

## Domain relations

- Date text -> a time value: many to one for the three forms (a time value has one `toString` and one `toUTCString` and one `toISOString`, and the parser accepts the three) - inferable (inferred from the sentences of decision 6 above). No engine entity is involved: the host model holds no `Date`.
## Change

Failing tests first: the tests below are written before the code and fail on the base (the module does not exist).

- `crates/num/src/date_text.rs` (new; `pub mod date_text;` in `crates/num/src/lib.rs`). `pub enum DateText { Invalid, Utc(f64), Local(f64) }` and `pub fn parse_date_text<U: Copy + Into<u32>>(text: &[U]) -> DateText`. `Utc(t)` is the unclipped time value of a form that fixes its instant (the date-only form read as UTC, a date-time with `Z` or an offset, the `toString` and `toUTCString` forms): the value is `MakeDate(MakeDay(year, month - 1, day), MakeTime(hour, minute, second, ms))` minus the offset in milliseconds (the offset taken off before the clip, so `+275760-09-13T01:00:00+01:00` is 8.64e15 once the caller clips); `Local(t)` is the date-time form with no offset, the fields read as a local time value, which the caller converts with `utc_from_local`; `Invalid` is NaN. The caller applies `time_clip`: an expanded year whose instant is out of range is NaN there. The grammar is the design's, item by item: first form `YYYY`, `YYYY-MM`, `YYYY-MM-DD`, then optionally `THH:mm`, `THH:mm:ss` or `THH:mm:ss.sss` and, after a time only, `Z`, `+HH:mm` or `-HH:mm`; a year of four digits or a sign and six digits, `-000000` invalid; MM 01 to 12; DD 01 to the length of that month in that year (`2021-02-30` is invalid, the design's own decision); HH 00 to 24 with 24 only when mm, ss and sss are 0 (the next day's 00:00); mm and ss 00 to 59; an offset's HH 00 to 23 and mm 00 to 59 (read by `parse_time_zone_offset_string`'s rules: this story does not call it for the `±HH:mm` of the first form, which has no seconds, and writes its own two-field read). Second form `Www Mmm DD Y HH:mm:ss GMT±HHmm` and an optional ` (name)` (a space, `(`, one or more code units other than `(` and `)`, `)` as the last code unit; ignored; the weekday read and not checked; Y four to six digits with a `-` for a year below 0 and `-0000` invalid). Third form `Www, DD Mmm Y HH:mm:ss GMT`. Anything else, a second space, a missing element or an extra code unit is `Invalid`. One pass, no backtracking, no allocation (the fields are `i64` and `u8`s on the stack); arithmetic on the fields is checked `i64` (a six-digit year is below 10^6).
- The writers: `date_string(&DateFields) -> String` (`DateString`, 21.4.4.41.2: `Www Mmm DD YYYY` with the year padded to four digits and a `-` only when negative; 271,821 and 275,760 have six digits), `time_string(&DateFields) -> String` (`HH:mm:ss`, `TimeString`, never 24), `time_zone_string(offset_ms: i64) -> String` (`GMT+hhmm` or `GMT-hhmm` from the offset in whole milliseconds as `offset_milliseconds` of the calendar story gives it: the sign, hours and minutes of its magnitude; the seconds of an offset are not written, as `TimeZoneString` writes only `hh` and `mm`; no name), `utc_string(&DateFields) -> String` (`Www, DD Mmm YYYY HH:mm:ss GMT`) and `iso_string(&DateFields) -> String` (`YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm:ss.sssZ`, the year as four digits for 0 to 9999 and else a sign and six digits). The tables of names are `const [&str; 7]` and `[&str; 12]`. Integer formatting is by hand (a small `push_padded(buf, value, width)`), no `format!` of a float, no allocation beyond the one `String` (at most 40 bytes, reserved first).
- Tests, in `crates/num/tests/date_text.rs` (new; the generated `tests/main.rs` gets `mod date_text;`): the accepted forms of each of the three grammars (at least four each, with the expected time value written out), every rejection the design lists (a second space, `2020-01-01Z`, `2021-02-30`, `2020-13-01`, `24:00:01`, `-000000`, `-0000`, a month name of the wrong case, an offset of `+24:00`, an extra code unit, the empty text, a lone sign), `24:00:00` as the next day, the name in parentheses (ignored; a `)` inside it is accepted up to the last code unit), the weekday not checked, `Local` for the date-time without an offset and `Utc` for the others, the boundary `+275760-09-13T00:00:00.000Z` (8.64e15) and `+275760-09-13T01:00:00+01:00` (8.64e15 after the offset), the writers over a table of 24 time values (written out, not computed) including a negative year, year 0, 9999, 10000 and the two extremes, `time_zone_string` of 0, 3,600,000, -19,800,000, 86,399,000 and -86,399,000, and a round trip: for each of the 24 time values `parse_date_text(utc_string)`, `parse_date_text(date_string + " " + time_string + " " + time_zone_string(0))` and `parse_date_text(iso_string)` give `Utc` of the value with the milliseconds dropped for the first two. A walk over 4,000 time values spread by the multiplicative step 2^40 + 7 over the whole range states its entry count and its time (expected under 0.3 s). Each test under 1.5 CPU seconds alone.
- The `f64` operations and casts of this story: none beyond the final `as f64` of an `i64` time value whose magnitude is below 2^53 (the Rust Reference, "Numeric cast": exact) and the `MakeDay`/`MakeTime`/`MakeDate` calls of the calendar story. No transcendental method.

## Context

Slice 10 brings jobs, `Promise`, `async` functions and generators and `for await` (`epic:slice-10-promises-and-async`; `docs/design/engine.md`, section 12, item 10; `feature Array.fromAsync 10` and `dir test/built-ins/Array/fromAsync/ 10` of the `swell-slices` block). This story brings `Array.fromAsync` (`#sec-array.fromasync`): an `async` builtin whose steps await the iterator results, the values of a sync iterable, the results of the mapper and the elements of an array-like, and settle a promise. It depends on `story:for-await-and-async-from-sync-iterator` (GetIterator with kind async, CreateAsyncFromSyncIterator, AsyncIteratorClose) and on `story:async-generators-and-their-request-queue` (5 cases of its list pass async generators as input), and on `story:async-methods-in-object-literals` (`async-iterable-input-does-not-await-input.js` declares `async next()` in an object literal); it reaches `story:async-functions-and-await` through them (69 cases use `async` functions and `asyncHelpers.js`). It needs no open story of slice 7: the 2 cases of `Array.fromAsync` that use `yield*` in an async generator are not on its list but in `docs/plan/slice-10/close-cross.cases.txt`, which the conformance close checks after `story:yield-star-in-async-generators`.

No refusal text names it: on the base `Array.fromAsync` is undefined, so a call of it is a `TypeError` of a call of undefined, and the runner counts its cases in slice 10 by `feature Array.fromAsync`. The list of this story is `docs/plan/slice-10/story-from-async.cases.txt`: the 73 cases of the key `from-async` of `docs/plan/slice-10/later.tsv` that need no `yield*` (the command is in the head of `docs/plan/slice-10/scan-later.awk`), all under `built-ins/Array/fromAsync`; 69 have the flag `async`. Design it builds on, by name (the text of the slice, merged at `843f178` and completed at `3648bf6`, whose line numbers are given): `docs/design/engine.md` decision 3, "`async` functions on suspended frames" (line 1101), items 2 to 5, and "A built-in async function" (line 1398), items 1 to 3, the answer to the question this story put to the design author (how a builtin awaits): the state of the steps lies in an internal kind, `ObjectKind::AsyncBuiltinActivation` (`object/async_builtin.rs`), with a null [[Prototype]] and never handed out, one kind for every built-in async function (its box holds one variant of fixed size per builtin; `AsyncDisposableStack.prototype.disposeAsync` of slice 15 is the next), charged with the record as owned bytes and credited by the sweep that frees it; the two functions of each Await hold it in their slots; a continuation costs 3 steps before the next of its steps runs and takes no frame and no re-entry of its own; each call it makes into code is a re-entry. Decision 4, "A built-in function with slots" (line 1636). `docs/design/host-interface.md` HOST-207 (line 1270: an Await of a built-in async function goes through the hooks of an `await`) and HOST-208 (line 1059: a built-in async function cut by a termination leaves its promise pending for good). No sentence is missing; the kind `FromAsyncActivation` of this story's first text is not the design's.

## What later stories reuse

The kind of a built-in async function, `ObjectKind::AsyncBuiltinActivation`, which no other builtin of this slice needs; `disposeAsync` of slice 15 adds its variant to the box.

## Domain relations

- `Array.fromAsync` call → its activation: one to one; the activation holds the state of the steps from the call until its promise settles, a termination ends it, or the sweep frees it once nothing that will resume it is reachable — inferable (inferred from ECMA-262 `#sec-array.fromasync` and `docs/design/engine.md` decision 3, "A built-in async function", item 1; no ess/1 document declares it: the host model `spec/domains/host.yaml` holds no engine value, and ESS does not restate the slots of ECMA-262, `AGENTS.md`, "Rules").

- Bounds ("A built-in async function", items 2 and 3). Steps: the call is the step of a builtin call and runs the steps to the first Await in it; each continuation costs 3 steps before the next of its steps runs (the job taken off, the call of the function the job calls, the continuation), and each call into code the steps of the code it runs; every turn of both loops awaits at least once, so an array-like of any length costs at least 3 steps per element and one without end ends at the step budget. Depth: a continuation takes no frame and no re-entry of its own, so a list of any length takes no depth; each call it makes into code (`next`, the mapper, a getter, a constructor) is a re-entry counted against `reentry_depth` and its frame is checked against `frame_depth`, and returns before the continuation does; a `RangeError` of a depth bound there is a throw completion of the call, which the steps handle as the draft's, IfAbruptCloseAsyncIterator in the branch of an iterable (`#sec-ifabruptcloseasynciterator`) and IfAbruptRejectPromise elsewhere, so the promise of the call is rejected with it; the interrupt is read at the continuation's step. Run loop: no instruction is added; the steps run in builtin functions of their own, so nothing is added to the frame of `Agent::execute` (`crates/swell/src/interp/dispatch.rs` line 244); `STACK_BYTES_PER_REENTRY` (`crates/swell/src/host/limits.rs` line 46) and the stack tests do not change and pass on seven eighths of `min_stack_bytes()`, or the unit gives the measured change of a re-entry from a continuation and the constant follows it. Memory: the activation and its box, the array and its growth (charged before it grows), the two functions of the pending Await with their boxes and two reactions; all freed once nothing that will continue it is reachable. Termination (item 3, HOST-208): a termination runs none of the steps, no AsyncIteratorClose and no rejection; one while its steps run (in its call, in a continuation or below a call into code) leaves its promise pending for good, the iterator not closed and the array with the elements defined so far; one while it waits keeps its state and a later checkpoint continues it, except when its job had started and a check of the continuation was refused, or `Agent::clear_microtasks` dropped the job.

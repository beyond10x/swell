The inline path of item 6 (line 1352): when the [[NextMethod]] is `%AsyncGeneratorPrototype%.next` of any realm of the agent and the iterator is an async generator object (for a received throw or return, when the method GetMethod finds is `%AsyncGeneratorPrototype%.throw` or `.return`), the instruction does what that builtin does (its promise, AsyncGeneratorValidate with a rejection, and, when the inner generator is suspended at its start or at a `yield`, the checks of item 4 and the move of a resumption, then the request queued), through the function of `story:async-generators-and-their-request-queue` that the builtin calls, and the inner frame runs above the delegating one on the same run loop; when the inner frame leaves the stack, the value it hands to the frame below is the promise of that request, which the delegating frame awaits. An inner generator that executes, drains or has completed is not resumed: the request is queued or settled as the builtin does and its promise handed over at once. Any other async iterator (an object of a script, an Async-from-Sync Iterator object) is called as the step of a `for await` calls `next`: a re-entry that returns before the delegating frame awaits. `crates/swell/src/interp/async_generator.rs`: the `yield` of a delegation, if the loop needs it apart from the plain `yield`, and the enqueue and resumption of a request made callable from the instruction where the builtin keeps them private.

- Bounds (item 6, line 1352). Steps: each turn of the loop is the call of the inner method and one `await` (3 steps when the loop resumes, item 4 of "`async` functions on suspended frames"), plus the `yield` of the value; a `next` through n levels of delegating async generators spends, per level, at least 4 and at most 20 steps more than through one level (101 levels: between 400 and 2,000 steps more), read with `last_entry_steps()` over the script and the checkpoint. Depth: while a `next` runs through a chain of async generators, its n levels hold n frames on the frame stack, counted against `frame_depth`, and take no Rust frame and no re-entry per level; past `frame_depth`, the check of item 4 at the level that would push rejects that level's request with the `RangeError` of "Depth bounds", which the delegating frame's `await` throws at its `yield*`, and the throw leaves each level outward, so the outermost request is rejected with that `RangeError` after the checkpoint; each job of the settling resumes one level and returns before the next job runs, one re-entry at a time whatever n. A delegation to any other async iterator calls it as one re-entry, counted against `reentry_depth`; a refusal throws the `RangeError` of "Depth bounds" from that call, which the delegating generator throws at its `yield*` as an abrupt completion of the call (and so rejects the request it answers when nothing catches it). Run loop: each new instruction's arm in `execute` (`crates/swell/src/interp/dispatch.rs` line 244) calls a function of its own (`#[inline(never)]` where its locals would grow the frame), so nothing is added to the frame of `Agent::execute`; the inline path pushes the inner frame on the frame stack and returns to the run loop, never calling `execute` again; `STACK_BYTES_PER_REENTRY` (`crates/swell/src/host/limits.rs` line 46) and the stack tests do not change and pass on seven eighths of `min_stack_bytes()`, or the unit gives the measured change and the constant follows it. Memory: between two `next` calls each level is suspended and takes neither depth; its frame, the functions and reactions of its `await` and its queued request are charged (items 1 and 2), so the number of levels is bounded by `frame_depth` and the memory budget. Termination: as item 5 of "Async generators on suspended frames" states for each level: a level whose frame is on the stack at the termination stays executing with its requests pending for good; a suspended level keeps its state.

Design it builds on: `docs/design/engine.md`, decision 3, "The iterator of `for`-`of`, of an array pattern and of a spread" (lines 654 to 717, merged on `wave/s3-1`), item 5, and "Iterators held by a builtin" (lines 611 to 652). An array pattern holds its record in the operand-stack entry of `story:for-of-statement` and closes it by the same `Finally` handler and close instruction, over the code of the pattern (the references of its targets, its steps, defaults and nested patterns): only a throw and its normal end close it, at its end the close instruction runs with a normal completion and closes when [[Done]] is false, a rest element leaves [[Done]] true, and a nested pattern has an entry and a handler of its own, inner first in the table, so a throw closes the inner iterator, then the outer one. A spread is one instruction that runs its iteration in Rust as a builtin does, with its record and the values it collects on the kept stack (`Agent::keep`), appending each value and charging one step per element through `charge_steps` beside the call of `next`; it runs its iterator to the end and never closes it, and needs no entry and no handler. A termination closes nothing. No new design.

On the base (`c6d44d1`) `crates/swell/src/builtins/number.rs` has `Number`, `Number.prototype.toString` and `valueOf` and the eight value properties (`VALUE_PROPERTIES`); `crates/swell/src/builtins/global.rs` has `isFinite`, `isNaN` and `eval` and the URI functions (`create_global_functions`); `swell-num` has `literal.rs` (`numeric_literal`, `string_to_number_trimmed`: the grammar of `StringToNumber`, hex, octal, binary, separators, `Infinity`) and `to_string.rs`. Design it follows, by name: `docs/design/engine.md` decision 6, the bullet "Parsing" (the decimal digits found are converted by `str::parse::<f64>`, which rounds to nearest, ties to even; swell does not take the draft's allowance to replace digits after the 20th by 0), "Which `f64` methods an engine crate may call"; decision 3 and its "Strings" rule (a String operation charges its code units before it reads them, one step per code unit past 32); ECMA-262 19.2.4, 19.2.5, 21.1.2.2 to 21.1.2.5, `StringToNumber` (`#sec-stringtonumber`, whose `StrWhiteSpace` the two functions use), `TrimString`.

**Prerequisite of the design author, not in the `Writes` of this story: the value of `parseInt` for a radix other than 10.** The draft says that for a radix that is not 2, 4, 8, 10, 16 or 32 the value of a string of more than 20 digits may be an approximation (19.2.5 step 16), and the design says nothing of `parseInt`. The sentence below is written into `docs/design/engine.md` before the unit starts, and the unit follows it as written; the start gate of the acceptance is its first line. The sentence:

> **parseInt for a radix other than 10** (decision 6, beside "Parsing"). `parseInt` converts a run of digits in radix 10 by `str::parse::<f64>` (correctly rounded, as a literal). In radix 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 it converts the run exactly and rounds once to nearest, ties to even (the leading 128 bits of the value in a `u128`, the lower digits as one sticky bit, the shift as the exponent). In every other radix it accumulates `acc * radix + digit` in `f64`, which is exact while `acc` is below 2^53 and an approximation after, as ECMA-262 19.2.5 step 16 allows; IEEE 754 multiplication and addition give the same result on every platform.

- `swell-num`, `crates/num/src/parse.rs` (new; `pub mod parse;` in `crates/num/src/lib.rs`). `parse_float(text)`: skip the leading `StrWhiteSpace` (the set `literal.rs` uses for `StringToNumber`: white space and line terminators), take the longest prefix that is a `StrDecimalLiteral` (21.1.2.12 reads 19.2.4 step 3: an optional sign, `Infinity`, or digits with an optional `.` and fraction, or `.` and digits, and an optional exponent `e` or `E` with an optional sign and digits; no hex, no octal, no separator `_`, and an exponent with no digit is left out of the prefix), and answer `0` with the sign kept for `-0`, the value of the prefix, or NaN when there is none. The digits are copied to an ASCII buffer reserved up front (at most the length of the text, checked by the builtin against the memory budget) and converted by `str::parse::<f64>`, which accepts only the ASCII forms the scanner already checked (`inf`, `nan` and `infinity` of Rust are never passed to it: `Infinity` is read by the scanner). `parse_int(text, radix)`: strip the whitespace, the sign, the radix rule of 19.2.5 steps 8 to 13 (`radix` 0 is 10, or 16 after `0x` or `0X`; a radix outside 2 to 36 is NaN; the prefix `0x` is stripped for 16 only), the longest run of digits valid in the radix, NaN when it is empty, the value by the method of the prerequisite above, and `-0` for a negative sign and a zero value (19.2.5 step 16: `-0` for a negative zero result). Each Rust operation used: `str::parse::<f64>` (correct rounding, `dec2flt`), `f64` `*` and `+` (IEEE 754), `u128` shifts and `leading_zeros`, `ldexp` by the exact power-of-two multiplication `x * 2^k` in at most two factors (`2^k` is a Number for k up to 1,023: the factors keep every intermediate normal), `copysign`. No transcendental method.

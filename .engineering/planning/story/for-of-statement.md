## Context

Slice 6 is delivered before slices 4 and 5 because it closes most of the first gap of the consumer, the testharness.js tests of the HTML parser of surf (`docs/plan/waves.md`, "The order after slice 3"): 191 refused constructs of slice 6 in `resources/testharness.js` and the scripts of `html/syntax/parsing/html5lib_url.html`, measured with the parser of `main` (`6a43e18`). Of the 191, 12 are `for`-`of` statements: 4 in `testharness.js` (lines 2550, 3540, 4553, 4582) and 8 in `html/syntax/parsing/resources/test.js` (lines 23, 75, 80, 85, 122, 171, 259, 334). This story lifts 10 of the 12; the other two have an array pattern in their head (`testharness.js` line 2550, `test.js` line 75) and parse once `story:array-destructuring-and-spread` has landed, which is the story after which both scripts parse with no refusal of slice 6 (the command is in that story). The first failing run of 210 cases of slice 6 ends on `for-of statement is not supported` (`awk -F'\t' '$2 == "06" && $5 != "Passed" && !($3 in s) { s[$3] = 1; if ($6 ~ /for-of statement is not supported/) c++ } END { print c }' .out/test262/runs.tsv` prints `210`; 602 runs over all slices end on it).

The base refuses the statement after its head is read, when `of` follows the first part (`crates/syntax/src/parser/statement.rs`, line 582, in the statement that `for_head` at line 565 begins); `story:let-const-and-block-scopes` keeps the refusal for `for (let x of ...)` and `for (const x of ...)`, and `story:object-destructuring` for an object pattern in the head. Fixtures that rely on it: `crates/syntax/tests/sources/refused/for-of.js` and `for-of-var.js`, removed.

The list of this story is `docs/plan/slice-06/story-for-of.cases.txt`: 257 failing cases (493 runs) of slice 6 and of the six directories `story:slice-03-conformance-close` moves to slice 6 (`test/built-ins/Array/from/`, `Array/prototype/keys/`, `values/`, `entries/`, `Object/fromEntries/`, `Object/groupBy/`), on no list of the first five stories of the slice, whose text and harness files use a `for`-`of` statement, no other part of slice 6 than those of the stories this one depends on, and no part of a later slice. Found by the token scan `docs/plan/slice-06/scan.awk` (run as `story:let-const-and-block-scopes` gives it, but with a copy of `meta.tsv` whose includes leave out `propertyHelper.js`, whose three later tokens are a branch behind `typeof`, a case of a bigint value and the name `checkSet`; the comment at the head of `lists-b.awk` gives the command; over the first failing run of each failing case of slice 6 and of those six directories on `main`, `6a43e18`) and the assignment `docs/plan/slice-06/lists-b.awk` (`gawk -v root=<checkout> -v meta=.cache/inventory/meta.tsv -f docs/plan/slice-06/lists-b.awk <scan output>`, the cases of the five lists of the first part taken out), which puts a case on the list of the first story of the slice that holds every part the case needs and whose own construct the case uses; 7 of its cases were placed by hand (`docs/plan/slice-06/hand-placed.tsv`: path, list, reason), the scan having marked them as of a later slice only for a later name in a string (`'Iterator'`). By directory: `language/statements/for-of` 252, `language/statements/for-in`, `language/statements/const`, `language/expressions/optional-chaining`, `language/expressions/assignmenttargettype` and `built-ins/Array` 1 each. A case of the list that ends on a part outside this story is reported with that part, and the coordinator takes it off the list by a commit of the list file on the wave branch whose body names that part; the acceptance reads the list as committed there when the wave closes.

Design it builds on: `docs/design/engine.md`, decision 3, "The iterator of `for`-`of`, of an array pattern and of a spread" (lines 654 to 717) and "Iterators held by a builtin" (lines 611 to 652), merged on `wave/s3-1`. The record is an entry of the frame's operand stack (`interp/frame.rs`, `Entry`, beside `Entry::Enumerator`, at the size of `Entry`) holding [[Iterator]], [[NextMethod]] and [[Done]], pushed by the head's GetIterator below the values of the head and the body and popped by the loop's exits, marked by `Frame::trace` (item 1). The step instruction calls `next` through `ops/iteration.rs` as a call of the agent's call path (a re-entry) and sets [[Done]] on a throw of `next`, `done` or `value` and on a result whose `done` is true; each iteration charges the steps of its instructions and its `LoopJump` (item 2). IteratorClose is compiled as a `try` with a `finally` is (`crates/compiler/src/statement.rs` `try_statement`; `crates/compiler/src/lib.rs` `Control::Finally` line 207, `exit_return` line 534, `exit_jump_to` line 641, `finally_dispatch`): a control entry of its own kind inside the loop's, a handler of the existing kind `Finally` over the code from the first step to the end of the body (the head's binding and pattern included) whose `items` keep the entry, `break`, `continue` and `return` leaving through it as through a `finally`, except that a `continue` of this loop does not pass it and a `break` of it does; its entry, outside the handler's range, is one instruction `[i, v, tag] -> [i, v, tag]` that, when [[Done]] is false, sets it and runs IteratorClose with a throw completion for `TAG_THROW` and a normal one for every other tag, so a throw of `return` on a `break`, `continue` or `return` goes to the handlers around the loop; no new handler kind (item 3). A termination enters no handler and calls no IteratorClose (item 4). The per-iteration bindings of `let` and `const` in the head follow item 3 of "Lexical bindings".

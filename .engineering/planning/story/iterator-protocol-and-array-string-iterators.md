## Context

Slice 6 is delivered before slices 4 and 5 because it closes most of the first gap of the consumer, the testharness.js tests of the HTML parser of surf (`docs/plan/waves.md`, "The order after slice 3"): 191 refused constructs of slice 6 in `resources/testharness.js` and the scripts of `html/syntax/parsing/html5lib_url.html`, measured with the parser of `main` (`6a43e18`). Of the 191, the 12 `for`-`of` statements, the 3 array patterns and the 1 spread element read an iterator: this story delivers the protocol they read and lifts none of them by itself (`story:for-of-statement` and `story:array-destructuring-and-spread` do). It is the one earlier story that gives the operations every later reader of an iterator shares (`for`-`of`, array patterns, spread, `Array.from`, `Object.fromEntries`, `Object.groupBy`), with their own tests, so that no two of those stories write them.

The base has no iterator: no `%IteratorPrototype%`, no `%ArrayIteratorPrototype%` or `%StringIteratorPrototype%`, no `Array.prototype.values`, `keys`, `entries` or `[Symbol.iterator]`, no `String.prototype[Symbol.iterator]`, and no `[Symbol.iterator]` on an arguments object (`crates/swell/src/object/arguments.rs`, module comment: "@@iterator, which both kinds define, comes with the iterators of slice 6"; `conformance/test262/sections.txt` line 93, `omit sec-arguments-exotic-objects symbol-iterator-property section-12-slice-6`, which the close of the slice removes as the one writer of that file). The directories `test/built-ins/Array/prototype/keys/`, `values/` and `entries/` are moved to slice 6 by `story:slice-03-conformance-close`.

The list of this story is `docs/plan/slice-06/story-iterators.cases.txt`: 56 failing cases (102 runs) of slice 6 and of the six directories `story:slice-03-conformance-close` moves to slice 6 (`test/built-ins/Array/from/`, `Array/prototype/keys/`, `values/`, `entries/`, `Object/fromEntries/`, `Object/groupBy/`), on no list of the first five stories of the slice, whose text and harness files use an iterator of an Array, a String or an arguments object, or `Symbol.iterator`, without `for`-`of`, a spread or an array pattern, no other part of slice 6 than those of the stories this one depends on, and no part of a later slice. Found by the token scan `docs/plan/slice-06/scan.awk` (run as `story:let-const-and-block-scopes` gives it, but with a copy of `meta.tsv` whose includes leave out `propertyHelper.js`, whose three later tokens are a branch behind `typeof`, a case of a bigint value and the name `checkSet`; the comment at the head of `lists-b.awk` gives the command; over the first failing run of each failing case of slice 6 and of those six directories on `main`, `6a43e18`) and the assignment `docs/plan/slice-06/lists-b.awk` (`gawk -v root=<checkout> -v meta=.cache/inventory/meta.tsv -f docs/plan/slice-06/lists-b.awk <scan output>`, the cases of the five lists of the first part taken out), which puts a case on the list of the first story of the slice that holds every part the case needs and whose own construct the case uses; 4 of its cases were placed by hand (`docs/plan/slice-06/hand-placed.tsv`: path, list, reason), the scan having marked them as of a later slice only for a later name in a string (`"Array Iterator"`, `"String Iterator"`). By directory: `built-ins/Array` 22, `built-ins/ArrayIteratorPrototype` 17, `built-ins/StringIteratorPrototype` 7, `built-ins/String` 5, `built-ins/Symbol` 3, `language/arguments-object` 2. A case of the list that ends on a part outside this story is reported with that part, and the coordinator takes it off the list by a commit of the list file on the wave branch whose body names that part; the acceptance reads the list as committed there when the wave closes.

Design it builds on: `docs/design/engine.md`, decision 3, "Iterators held by a builtin" (lines 611 to 652, merged on `wave/s3-1`), and for the readers in the frame "The iterator of `for`-`of`, of an array pattern and of a spread" (lines 654 to 717). Rust code holds the record as an engine type, `IteratorRecord` ([[Iterator]], [[NextMethod]], [[Done]], handles only) in `crates/swell/src/ops/iteration.rs`; a builtin keeps [[Iterator]], [[NextMethod]] and every value it still needs across a call on the agent's kept stack (`Agent::keep`, `Agent::release` to its mark on every way out, `crates/swell/src/object/builtin_function.rs` lines 160 and 167), so `test-collect` fails one that leaves a value out (item 1). Every call of `next` and of `return` goes through the agent's call path, which charges the call's step and counts against `reentry_depth` and, for an ECMAScript `next`, `frame_depth`; the operations of `ops/iteration.rs` charge no step of their own (item 2). The answer to whether a `next` that is itself a builtin is charged: yes, the step of its call by the call path, and a builtin's loop over an iterator charges one more step per iteration through `charge_steps` (`crates/swell/src/host/agent.rs` line 559) whatever `next` is, since `%ArrayIteratorPrototype%.next` runs no instruction (item 3). IteratorClose takes the completion it is given and never `Abrupt::Terminated` (item 4).

## Domain relations

- Array Iterator → the iterated object, many-to-one, the iterator keeps it alive until it is done, then drops it ([[IteratedArrayLike]] set to undefined) — inferable (`#sec-%arrayiteratorprototype%.next`; no ess/1 document declares it).
- String Iterator → the iterated string, many-to-one, the same — inferable (`#sec-%stringiteratorprototype%.next`).

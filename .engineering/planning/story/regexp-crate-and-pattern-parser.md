## Context

Slice 5 (`epic:slice-05-regexp`) is delivered next, by what the first consumer needs (`docs/plan/waves.md`, "The order after slice 6"): the testharness.js tests of the HTML parser of surf parse with no refusal of slice 6, and of slice 5 there remain 12 regular expression literals, which parse and whose evaluation is refused (`crates/syntax/src/error.rs` line 26, `REGEXP_REFUSAL`). `docs/plan/slice-05/consumer-regexps.tsv` lists them by file and line (10 in `resources/testharness.js`, 1 in `html/syntax/parsing/resources/template.js`, 1 in `html/syntax/parsing/resources/test.js`) with the constructs of each and the call made on it. None uses a flag but `g`; two (`testharness.js` line 581, `template.js` line 68) write `{` and `}` as pattern characters, which only the grammar of Annex B (B.1.2) admits. The first wave of the slice makes the 12 evaluate and those calls answer, in six stories: this one and `story:string-replace-replace-all-and-split` side by side, then `story:regexp-literal-early-errors` and `story:regexp-compiler-and-backtracking-matcher` side by side, then `story:regexp-objects-exec-and-literals`, then `story:regexp-symbol-methods-and-string-match-search`. This story lifts none of the 12 by itself; it gives the next three the parser.

The crate `swell-regexp` does not exist (`docs/design/engine.md`, section 2: "`swell-regexp` and `swell-cli` are not created yet"; "its first story, `story:regexp-crate-and-pattern-parser`, creates it"). This story creates it with the pattern parser, its early errors, and the trait through which the crate charges its work: the crate names no type of `swell-syntax` or `swell` (decision 2), so it cannot name `swell_syntax::Ledger` (`crates/syntax/src/ledger.rs`). No test262 case runs on this crate alone; the cases of the grammar are on the list of `story:regexp-literal-early-errors`. This story builds the grammar of B.1.2, and the cases under `test/annexB/` stay counted in slice 18 (the block line `dir test/annexB/ 18`) until `story:slice-05-conformance-close` decides them.

Design it builds on: decision 7 of `docs/design/engine.md` (the grammar of 22.2.1 and of B.1.2 in this slice), with its paragraphs "How `swell-regexp` charges its work" (the trait of five operations, item 1), "What one step of the matcher is" (the parser charges no step; RegExpInitialize charges one per code unit of the pattern before ParsePattern; the parser polls the interrupt at least once per 4,096 code units read and per 4,096 nodes visited), "No Rust recursion in the parser and the compiler of a pattern" and "Refusals until the later stories of slice 5"; and decision 3, "Depth bounds" (the nesting of a pattern counts against `parser_nesting` from zero at its start, and passing it is the `SyntaxError` naming `parser_nesting`, HOST-142 and HOST-143).

- `src/budget.rs`: the trait the crate charges through, with the five operations of decision 7 ("How `swell-regexp` charges its work", item 1): charge bytes before the growth they pay for, or refuse and charge nothing; credit bytes charged before; grant steps, asked for 1 to 4,096 at a time, which spends and answers the smaller of the number asked and the steps left, at least 1, or refuses when no step is left, and reads the interrupt first; return steps granted and not run; poll the interrupt. A refusal is a stop of the crate: over the memory budget, over the step budget, or interrupted; after a stop the crate credits everything it holds and answers the stop, never an error of the pattern. Beside it a type that charges nothing and never stops, for tests and tools, as `swell_syntax::Unlimited`. Growth is by capacity, as `Meter::grow` does (`crates/syntax/src/ledger.rs`, "By capacity"): a vector doubles to at least 4 elements with `try_reserve_exact`, and a refusal of the allocator is a refused charge. The parser of this story charges and credits bytes and polls; the grant and the return are for the matcher of `story:regexp-compiler-and-backtracking-matcher`, which this story does not write.
- `src/flags.rs`: the flags of a pattern from their code units (`#sec-regexpinitialize`, the list `d g i m s u v y`): an error for an unknown or repeated flag and for `u` with `v` (`#sec-parsepattern`, step 1).
- `src/ast.rs`, `src/parser.rs`, `src/early.rs`, `src/error.rs`: ParsePattern (`#sec-parsepattern`) of a pattern given as UTF-16 code units with its flags and a nesting limit, answering the tree or one error with its offset. The grammar of 22.2.1 (`#sec-patterns`) with its parameters [UnicodeMode], [UnicodeSetsMode] and [NamedCaptureGroups]: disjunctions and alternatives; the assertions `^`, `$`, `\b`, `\B`, lookahead and lookbehind, positive and negative; the quantifiers `*`, `+`, `?`, `{n}`, `{n,}`, `{n,m}`, greedy or lazy; the atoms (pattern characters, `.`, escapes, classes, groups capturing, named and non-capturing, modifiers `(?ims-ims:...)`); the escapes (decimal escapes as backreferences, `\k<name>`, `\d \D \s \S \w \W`, control escapes, `\cX`, `\0`, `\xHH`, `\uHHHH`, `\u{...}` and the surrogate pair of two `\u` escapes under `u`, identity escapes); class ranges and class escapes. With neither `u` nor `v` the grammar is that of B.1.2 (`#sec-regular-expressions-patterns`): ExtendedAtom with `{` and `}` as pattern characters and InvalidBracedQuantifier, ExtendedPatternCharacter, the quantifiable lookahead, legacy octal escapes, ClassControlLetter, the IdentityEscape of B.1.2 (`\k` an identity escape in a pattern with no group name), and class ranges with a class escape at an end (B.1.2.1). A pattern with a GroupName is parsed again with [+NamedCaptureGroups] (ParsePattern, step 3). The early errors of `#sec-patterns-static-semantics-early-errors`: a quantifier range whose minimum passes its maximum, a backreference past the count of groups, `\k` naming no group, a group name declared twice except in different alternatives (`regexp-duplicate-named-groups`), a class range whose ends are out of order or a class escape under `u`, a modifier repeated, in both lists, or the empty `(?-:`; and those of `#sec-patterns-static-semantics-early-errors-annexb`. The tree counts the capturing groups and keeps the group names; the pattern text itself is kept by the caller (for `source`, `story:regexp-objects-exec-and-literals`).
- Refused in this story, each a refusal of slice 5 (decision 7, "Refusals until the later stories of slice 5": the message exactly `<construct> is not supported before slice 5`, which `swell_syntax::message_kind` reads as a refusal, `crates/syntax/src/error.rs` line 46), each with a test here that it is refused and lifted by the story named: the flag `v` (`class set expressions of the v flag`, `story:regexp-v-flag-class-sets-and-properties-of-strings`); a property escape `\p{...}` or `\P{...}` (`property escape`, `story:regexp-property-escapes-and-property-tables`); a group name with a code point outside ASCII, written or escaped (`group name outside ASCII`, `story:identifiers-and-group-names-outside-ascii`). A grammar error and the nesting limit are not refusals. The flag `i` and a modifier `i` are not refused here: the compile step refuses them (`story:regexp-compiler-and-backtracking-matcher`), so a pattern with `i` and an early error answers the early error.
- Bounds (decision 7, "No Rust recursion in the parser and the compiler of a pattern"): a loop with an explicit stack, not recursion counted against the limit. The parser keeps the open levels on a stack of its own, one entry per open group (capturing, named or not capturing), lookaround and modifier group (and under `v` per nested class, which `story:regexp-v-flag-class-sets-and-properties-of-strings` adds), charged through the trait before it grows; the level that would pass the limit the caller passes (the host value `parser_nesting`), counted from zero at the start of the pattern, is the error `nesting deeper than parser_nesting (<limit>)`, the text `swell_syntax::message_kind` reads as the nesting limit (`crates/syntax/src/error.rs` line 29; the crate writes the text itself, since it cannot name that constant). It is not a refusal. The tree's nodes live in vectors and name their children by index, so dropping the tree does not recurse. Every node and list of the tree is charged before it grows and credited when the tree is dropped; the parser charges no step and polls the interrupt at least once per 4,096 code units it reads and per 4,096 nodes it visits; on a stop it credits what it holds and answers the stop.
